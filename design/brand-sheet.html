Brand direction

One name, one mark, no endorsement line. Every colour below has been measured against the background it sits on; the ratios are printed on the swatches and enforced by a test that fails the build.

Recommended — Marela
Marela

Everything they need to know, in one place they can ask.

ma-RE-la. Coined, so it means nothing and carries nothing. Three even syllables, no ambiguous letter: an English speaker and a French speaker say it the same way on sight, and either one, hearing it, spells it the same way. It reads as a person's name without being a common one — which matters, because the caregiver is going to ask it questions. No app, no company and no trademark surfaced in any register searched.

Second — Nolera
Nolera

Tout ce qu'il faut savoir, au même endroit.

no-LE-ra. Calmer and more clinical than Marela, slightly more premium, and the more obviously ownable of the two. The only holder found anywhere is a Japanese company registered in July 2026 in Hokkaido with no products and no web presence. Choose this one if you want the brand to feel like an instrument rather than a person.

The rest of the shortlist — all four cleared the same screen
Nemba

NEM-ba. The phonetically bulletproof one — the mb closes the first syllable, so the e is locked to the same sound in both languages with no room to drift. Five letters, best of the six on an app icon. Blunter and less warm than Marela. Two one-person consultancies use the word; neither holds a mark.

Tanela

ta-NE-la. The softest of the six, and the closest in feel to Marela if you want warmth without the person-name reading. Held by a Lithuanian metal fabricator and a dormant Panama holding company — neither adjacent, neither with search presence worth competing against.

Tovela

to-VE-la. Pleasant, and the least distinctive of the six — it sits close enough to Tanela that it is really an alternate of it rather than a separate option. One micro-business uses it, and only because it is the founder's surname.

Kembo

KEM-bo. Short and punchy, with the same locked vowel as Nemba. Clear on the App Store. Reads more like a product than a person, which cuts against the whole point — that a caregiver speaks to it in a sentence.

Out, with the reason. Meanwhile — good English word, unusable in French: no equivalent, the wh cluster does not exist in French phonology, and it cannot be spelled from hearing it. Relais — Relay runs around 1,000 licensed tobacco outlets in French stations and airports. Carnet — crowded and near-unregistrable in the one market where it is strongest. Nella — an AI bedtime-story app for children. Valma — a registered UK mark for household cleaning products. Aluna, Venna, Orla, Nerola, Nimbo — live apps or software marks under the exact name.

Every value here is the manifest's, moved only in lightness and only as far as its contrast target required. Lisette blue shifted 2.5%, Jeannette petrol 0.2% — neither is visible side by side. The ground, the card and raspberry did not move at all.

Raspberry keeps its exact value because darkening it to 4.5:1 produced #C22C59 — a different colour, and close enough to the safety red to blur completion into danger. It is instead restricted to large filled shapes, where 3:1 is the correct target, and design/contrast.mjs enforces that restriction so a future use as small text fails the build.

The red is reserved for allergies, medication and emergency contacts and is used nowhere else. A colour that appears on ordinary content stops carrying weight, and the one screen where it matters gets skimmed.

All six clear 4.5:1 on the ground, so they can carry text rather than only decorate. Each is permanently paired with a symbol, because these hues are confusable under deuteranopia — and a parent choosing "the blue one" for a child with a nut allergy should not be relying on the caregiver having full colour vision.

The indigo is louder than the rest of the palette and stays that way. Muting it toward the beige collapses it against the petrol under simulated deuteranopia; its saturation is doing accessibility work rather than decoration.

Typography
Newsreader — brand and headings only
Chez les Martin
The manifest's serif. Bookish without being decorative, full French diacritics. Used for the wordmark and page titles, never for body copy or interface labels. Serif headings are close to absent in this category, which is most of why they read as considered rather than generic.
Figtree — everything else, two sizes
Sleeping bags: 2.5 tog
The interface sets at 15px. Guide content and safety-critical facts set at 16px, through a separate --text-read token. The chrome can afford to be compact; the sentences a caregiver is decoding in a second language at 2am cannot, and shrinking those to tidy up the layout optimises the screenshot over the reader.
